> **Release candidate.** This is the fourth candidate for beta.8, published so it can be deployed and
> measured live on a household install. It is a prerelease: `latest` stays on beta.7, and the final
> `v0.2.0-beta.8` tag follows once the gates below are verified live.
>
> **Changes since rc.3:**
> - **Black letterbox bars on 4K HDR (#1673).** On Intel GPUs, a 4K HDR source shorter than 16:9 aired
>   with green bars above and below the picture, because the 10-bit pad wrote zeros. The pad now runs
>   in OpenCL and writes true black. The picture itself is unchanged.
> - **Watching a channel no longer changes what's on air (#1674).** A tune-in could make the scheduler
>   re-sort the deck mid-window and replace the programme already airing, so the guide stopped
>   matching the picture. Airing history is now read as it stood when the window opened, and per
>   episode instead of per series.
> - **No more mid-episode cut once a day (#1675).** Channels whose pool is longer than their window
>   cut the programme on air at 00:00 UTC (early evening in the Americas) and joined the next one
>   part-way in. The programme crossing the boundary now plays to its end, and windows turn on the
>   guide's timezone.
> - **Channels air more of their filler (#1684).** Breaks stopped filling at the tightest duration
>   rung, so a channel aired only a few of its matched clips. They now top up from the rest of the
>   ladder: on the demo library, distinct clips per channel rose from 4 to 15.
> - **Filler supply.** Untitled clips are named from their transcript or on-screen text, with no LLM
>   needed (#1452). Scheduled fetching prefers the eras and bookends that channels can't fill, and
>   readiness reports each coverage gap's yield (#749). The guide assembles a channel's breaks in one
>   batch, about 7× faster (#1420). Keyframe windows on 24 fps and older sources return every frame
>   (#1488).
> - **Settings apply without a restart (#1659).** The diagnostics folder and the two long-reel files
>   now apply live. Only settings that truly need one (the filler folders, the database URL and the
>   listen address) still show the restart notice, and only to admins.
> - **The first pieces of the new design (#1659).** Dashboard is now **Home**. The setup wizard's step
>   list goes back to steps you've already reached. The search hint names Ctrl K outside Apple devices.
>
> **Changes in rc.3:** the watermark returns on Intel GPUs through an OpenCL kernel (#1613); watermark
> settings with a 40% default and four looks (#1617); analog-style channel switching with a dial-set
> sound in the web player (#1620); compound TV genres match their parts, so those channels go live
> (#1630); and the reorganized docs (#1572).
>
> **Changes in rc.2 (#1595):** the watermark self-check measures speed and turns off a GPU overlay
> that can't keep up, and a cold tune seeks from the indexed keyframe.
>
> **Beta scope: on-demand playout (#1512).** Channels are no longer encoded ahead of time. Prepared
> media is gone: no encoder runs while nobody is watching, and a tune starts one transcode for that
> channel. A long-lived channel packager owns each channel's timeline. It serves 1 s fMP4 HLS to the
> Web and TV apps and continuous MPEG-TS to media-server tuners from the same encode, with no format
> change at programme ↔ commercial boundaries (G1, G4).
>
> - **Resource-aware admission (G5).** A ResourceBudget sizes playout from measured capacity (GPU,
>   encoder sessions and the container's CPU allowance) and prices each stream by its source class.
>   On slow CPU-only hosts a software ladder steps quality down by measured speed instead of refusing.
>   Filler work is capped and yields to playback.
> - **Uniform output (G6, G7).** Every channel airs H.264 with AAC stereo in one fixed geometry, from
>   media files read directly. Stream facts, keyframe index and loudness come from Loomarr's own
>   measurements, never from the media server at airtime. Live loudnorm is replaced by static gain.
> - **Commercial breaks** air mid-programme at measured scene fades, on by default, with a
>   per-channel off switch.
> - **4K (G10).** Channels with meaningful 4K content offer a premium HEVC format, 4K SDR or 4K
>   HDR10, alongside the 1080p SDR baseline. The master playlist lists it, and only clients that can
>   decode it take it. Every other client keeps the baseline.
> - **Tone mapping (G11).** HDR sources are tone-mapped on the GPU with an install-wide curve
>   (`playout.tone_curve`, Hable by default). The image ships the GPU tone-map runtime, and a
>   startup self-check fails loudly if the picture comes out black.
> - **Surfing (G3).** Neighbouring channels are warmed speculatively, and a cold channel shows its
>   latest still until the first frame arrives.
> - **ffmpeg 9.** The image ships FFmpeg n9.0.1 (BtbN `n9.0.1-11-ge47273f4d9`). Its corresponding
>   source is published in
>   [third-party-sources-2026-09-27](https://github.com/loomarr/loomarr/releases/tag/third-party-sources-2026-09-27);
>   the other third-party sources are unchanged from
>   [third-party-sources-2026-08-31](https://github.com/loomarr/loomarr/releases/tag/third-party-sources-2026-08-31).
>
> **Known limitations:**
> - Surfing (G3) and boundary smoothness (G4) are not yet certified on a real Shield. The TV
>   numbers so far come from the Android TV emulator (#1037).
> - The 24 h soak (G4: fewer than 1 stall per viewer-hour) has not run yet.
> - The premium 4K HDR10 path has not yet been watched on a real HDR TV (the Shield).
> - On large film channels with a regular viewer, next-day repeats rose after the window boundary
>   moved to local midnight (#1694, under investigation before the final tag).
