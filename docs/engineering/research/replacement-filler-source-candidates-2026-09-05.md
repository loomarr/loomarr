## Rights boundary used for every rank

LOC's National Screening Room says that it is unaware of restrictions for the vast majority of its motion pictures, but makes assessment the user's responsibility and expressly identifies privacy, publicity, licence, trademark, donation, US, and foreign-rights risk ([collection rights statement](https://www.loc.gov/collections/national-screening-room/about-this-collection/rights-and-access/)). That statement is availability and a useful first-party rights notice, not a provider-processing grant.

For a US-government work, [17 USC 105](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title17-section105&num=0&edition=prelim)/public-domain reasoning is only a starting presumption: confirm that the item was actually prepared by a federal employee within official duties. Contractors, coproducers, grantees, donated works, music, performance, trademarks/insignia, and identifiable people are separate rights questions. CDC specifically distinguishes agency material from third-party material and asks reusers to seek permission where needed ([CDC policy](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html)); NASA permits much reuse but separately restricts its identifiers and flags people, third-party content, endorsement, and AI use ([NASA images and media policy](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)). A public-domain US work also does not itself establish the global, sublicensable provider-processing authority required by Loomarr.

`download-now` below therefore means: direct moving-media availability plus sufficiently concrete first-party metadata to create a **local acquisition candidate**, not approval, redistribution, or a claim that the work is safe for model/provider use. `inspect-first` means a precise item exists but an unresolved rights, role, or ancestry issue blocks download. `reject/not-fit` means the source itself supplies the reason not to use it.

## Collection-boundary assessment

* **LOC National Screening Room:** best immediate local research pool. Item pages expose video, metadata and a collection/item rights notice. Prefer specific collection-PD notices (as for *Market Street*) and pre-1931 US publications, but retain all LOC caveats and screen offensive content. LOC possession is not creator ownership or a provider grant.
* **NARA:** use the official Catalog only to resolve exact archival descriptions and linked digital objects. NARA records may describe federal records, but record custody does not settle third-party, contractor, donor, publicity, trademark, or worldwide processing rights. Do not take a bulk/archive download.
* **CDC/HHS:** agency-authored material can be a local PSA supplement only after exact item ownership and exceptions are checked. CDC's own policy rules out treating partner material as federal PD. SAMHSA campaign-level language is not an unlimited commercial/modification/provider licence.
* **NASA:** NASA media can be a useful promo/trailer *discovery* pool, but insignia/endorsement, identifiable persons, third-party content and NASA's AI guidance are blocking attributes until an exact-item review. It is not a bumper shortcut.
* **NHTSA/Ad Council:** NHTSA campaign restrictions and Ad Council placement-only terms are affirmative rejection evidence, not a permission gap to assume away.
* **Internet Archive/Prelinger:** Archive is a delivery host and collection description authority, not proof of underlying ownership. Accept only an exact item whose item-page licence and original-producer chain meet the required use; CC BY/BY-SA and US-PD status do not independently convey provider-processing permission.
* **Met Open Access:** useful for still-object enrichment only; irrelevant to this moving-media-parent replacement task.

## Acquisition protocol, query routes, and manifest

Use these exact no-download discovery routes first:

1. LOC JSON search: `https://www.loc.gov/film-and-videos/?fo=json&at=results,pagination&c=100&q=<quoted-title>`; preserve `id`, `resources[].files`, `dates`, `contributor`, `partof`, and `rights` from the returned item before following an item download selector.
2. LOC collection query: `https://www.loc.gov/collections/national-screening-room/?fo=json&q=<quoted-title>` and, for the explicit-PD San Francisco pool, `https://www.loc.gov/collections/before-and-after-the-great-earthquake-and-fire-in-san-francisco-1897-to-1916/?fo=json&q=<title>`.
3. NARA: `https://catalog.archives.gov/api/v2/records/search?q=<quoted-title>&rows=100` (or the human-facing URLs above). Record the returned NAID and the exact digital-object URL; reject ambiguous title-only matches.
4. NASA: `https://images.nasa.gov/search?q=<terms>&media_type=video`; retain the NASA ID, centre, creator, caption, keywords, and exact download/stream URL.
5. CDC/SAMHSA: begin at the first-party campaign/search URL, then preserve the exact item-page URL and embedded direct-media URL; a campaign landing page is not a manifest item.
6. Prelinger: use the item page/API metadata (`https://archive.org/metadata/<identifier>`) only to identify a candidate; preserve its licence field, uploader, original producer, file list and rights notes. Do not treat it as an ownership API.

Create a pre-download manifest row for every proposed file with at least:

```text
candidate_id, role_claim, title, exact_item_url, direct_media_url, owning_institution,
creator_or_producer, producing_agency, publication_date, duration_catalogued_seconds,
moving_media_evidence, collection, catalogue_id_or_naid, source_host,
rights_statement_url, rights_text_snapshot_sha256, rights_basis, rights_uncertainty,
third_party_flags, trademark_insignia_flags, privacy_publicity_flags,
provider_processing_status, programme_parent_identity, parent_provenance_key,
creator_family_key, campaign_family_key, v5_named_parent_screen_result,
hash_family_clearance_status, reviewer, reviewed_at, disposition, stop_reason
```

`v5_named_parent_screen_result` can be `provenance-screened`; `hash_family_clearance_status` must remain `not-run` until media has been acquired through an approved workflow. Never populate either field from title similarity alone.

## Recommended order and hard stop conditions

1. Build manifest rows and rights snapshots for programme rows 1–4; acquire no media until the owner approves the manifest.
2. Resolve and review LOC rows 5–10 and NARA rows 11–13 one at a time. Stop counting any item that shares creator/producer/campaign/family with an already selected parent until the holdout's family gate adjudicates it.
3. Run the required pre-download parent/provenance screen against all six named v5 LOC parents. Then have the authorized acquisition workflow perform file hash/family checks; this research report cannot do that.
4. Treat the anchor gap as an outreach task immediately: obtain a signed, whole-work grant from four independent broadcasters/rightsholders for bumpers, six PSA owners, and four trailer/promotional owners. Do not let a public player substitute for that grant.
5. Stop local archival acquisition if fewer than ten distinct programme-parent candidates survive exact-record, 120-second, rights, content, and family review; stop any provider path without an explicit worldwide, transferable/sublicensable processing grant; stop any item with unresolvable contractor, music, talent, trademark, privacy, or parent-identity ambiguity.

The decisive recommendation is therefore: use LOC's early, item-traceable pool to make a small local-only parent buffer, but make direct rights-complete contributions the delivery path for all missing bumper/PSA/trailer roles and for any provider-facing corpus.
