## Start here

The packages under `internal/` group into the areas below. Each area has its own generated page listing its packages, what each one imports, how many packages import it, and which other areas it reaches into (counted as package-to-package imports), each with the reason. The area table lives in `cmd/arch-docs/areas.go`; a new package must be added to one area before it merges, and a new import into another area must be declared there with its reason. A reason ending "until …" is an import that should go.

### [Library](areas/library.md)

What the household owns and wants. Talks to the media server (Emby or Jellyfin), keeps Loomarr's own record of every film and episode, looks titles up on TMDB, and asks Sonarr or Radarr to fetch a title the household does not have yet. The metadata providers decide which source's facts about a file win.

### [Media](areas/media.md)

Shared tools for looking at and working on media files: reading what a file's header says, measuring keyframes and loudness, running ffmpeg and ffprobe safely, picking the GPU encoder, and the one pipeline every image goes through. It also holds the checks that judge a clip's picture, sound and level, and whether two adverts are the same one.

### [Channels](areas/channels.md)

Channels and what they play. A channel's lineup becomes an ordered schedule, and the reconcile engine keeps each channel's programming current for whichever backend plays it. Operator edits and approved proposals each have one write path. Self-updating channels are re-curated here, Home's channel ideas come from here, and so does who is watching now.

### [Proposals](areas/proposals.md)

Turning "I want a channel that feels like X" into a lineup a person can approve. The suggester checks every title against the real library and catalog, the workflow tracks each request through to the household's first channel, and the outlook explains a pending proposal before anyone approves it. Accepting a proposal, by a person's approval or the auto-approve grant within its pending-acquisition quota, is its own package. The quality and planner-reference packages measure how good the suggestions are.

### [Playout](areas/playout.md)

Getting a channel onto a TV. Loomarr's own streaming engine stitches a channel into one continuous stream and burns in the channel's logo; Tunarr is the other backend that can play a channel. The Live TV wiring and the backend transition publish channels to the media server, LAN discovery lets TV apps find Loomarr, and the bench measures the pipeline on real hardware.

### [Filler](areas/filler.md)

Commercials, bumpers and station IDs. Sources such as YouTube, archive.org and local folders are listed and fetched; every clip is checked for safety, structure and defects, then admitted or refused; admitted clips are catalogued with their tags and the TV stations they name; and the catalog is assembled into the pods that fill a channel's breaks.

### [People](areas/people.md)

Who may use Loomarr and how they are reached: sign-in sessions, invitations, password recovery, contact addresses and notifications, plus the encryption of the secrets Loomarr keeps in its database.

### [Platform](areas/platform.md)

The ground everything else stands on: the store and its two databases, settings and start-up configuration, background jobs and scheduled clean-up, the activity feed and diagnostics, metrics, the outbound HTTP client, the event bus, disk-space reservations, the language-model and OpenRouter clients, the embedded web app, release tooling, and small shared vocabularies such as holidays, language codes, the audience rating ladder and whole-word text matching.

### [Edge](areas/edge.md)

Where the outside world comes in. The api package is Loomarr's HTTP surface, and app is the composition root that builds every other area and hands them to it. `api/httpcore` is the plumbing every part of the HTTP surface shares: problem responses, request ids, roles and the route-role marker. `app/playoutresolve` is the composition root's playout wiring: it reads the stores and settings that `playout` must not know about.

### [Test support](areas/test.md)

Code that exists for tests and never ships in the server: the shared test doubles and fixtures, the store's SQLite and Postgres test databases, the invented demo library and the TMDB stand-in it and the tests share, the end-to-end integration journeys, and the evaluation harness that runs only under its own build tag.

## Two flows, end to end

### From a request to Live TV

A member asks for a channel through `api`. `proposals/workflow` turns the request into a durable Proposal Job and runs `proposals/suggest`, which grounds each title through `library/titlesearch`(the library, `library/tmdb` and the clip catalog) and `proposals/webreference`, using a model from `platform/llm`, and returns the values in `proposals`. When a person approves it, `channels/approval` plans the channel and `channels/edit` writes it through `platform/store`; any title the household lacks becomes a `library/provision` record that `library/provisionbackstop` hands to `library/requester` for Sonarr or Radarr. `channels/schedule` turns the approved lineup and what is actually available into ordered programming, and the `channels` reconcile engine keeps that current for the channel's backend: either Loomarr's own `playout` engine, whose `playout/packager` stitches one continuous stream, or `playout/tunarr`. `playout/livetvpublish` registers the playlist and guide with the media server's Live TV through `library`, and when the household changes backend, `playout/backendtransition` prepares the new one fully before publishing it to the media server.

### From a filler source to a break

An operator registers a source; `filler/source` lists what it offers, with `filler/source/youtube` and `filler/source/archive` as the remote kinds. `filler/fetch` downloads each pulled clip into the drop folder, and `filler` drains that folder into the clip folder, while `filler/records` records the sources, pulls and acquisition runs. Each clip is then checked: `filler/screening` for what it says and shows, and whether it is safe to air, `filler/structure/assessment` for long reels that need splitting, and `filler/defectfloor` for picture and sound defects. A clip that passes its checks enters the catalog, whose values are `filler/clips`, whose tags are `filler/taxonomy`, and which `platform/store/clipstore` persists. When `channels/schedule` leaves a break in a channel's programming, the `channels` engine asks `filler` for a pod that fits it, and `playout` plays the pod as part of the stream.

## How the area pages are made

Generated recursively from each package's own doc comment and exact internal imports, using full paths below `internal/`, so they cannot drift from the code the way a hand-maintained list does. Sizes are deliberately absent — they change on nearly every commit, which would make the drift check red by default and train everyone to regenerate without reading.
